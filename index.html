Research Overview

My research program develops artificial intelligence systems that fundamentally accelerate scientific discovery by bridging the gap between data-driven learning and domain-specific knowledge. I pursue this vision through two complementary research thrusts that address critical bottlenecks in both computational and experimental science:

Thrust 1: Physics-Constrained Machine Learning for Scientific Simulation

Problem

Current deep learning approaches for scientific simulation often violate fundamental physical laws, producing physically implausible results that limit their utility in real-world applications. While neural operators can learn powerful mappings from data, they frequently fail to respect conservation laws, symmetry principles, and other domain constraints that make physical systems predictable and interpretable.

Approach

I develop provably constrained probabilistic operator models that embed physical constraints directly into neural architectures through mathematical guarantees rather than soft penalties. By designing models where conservation laws (mass, energy, momentum) are enforced by construction, I create AI systems that learn from physical systems while remaining fundamentally faithful to the underlying physics.

  • Architectural Guarantees: Designing neural networks that inherently satisfy PDE constraints through careful architectural choices
  • Probabilistic Modeling: Incorporating uncertainty quantification while maintaining physical consistency
  • Multi-Scale Learning: Bridging scales from microscopic interactions to macroscopic phenomena

Applications

Climate modeling, fluid dynamics, materials design, and renewable energy systems—domains where physical consistency is non-negotiable and traditional simulations are computationally prohibitive.

Thrust 2: Closed-Loop Active Design for Genome-wide Perturb-seq

Problem

Genome-scale Perturb-seq screens, which measure transcriptomic responses to genetic perturbations, are revolutionizing functional genomics but remain prohibitively expensive. Profiling a full library of perturbations requires millions of cells and substantial financial resources, creating a fundamental accessibility barrier for most research labs and limiting the scale of biological discovery.

Approach

I create closed-loop AI systems that actively design optimal perturbation experiments by sequentially selecting the most informative genetic perturbations to profile. My framework combines optimal transport theory with information-theoretic acquisition functions to maximize learning efficiency under strict experimental budgets.

  • Optimal Transport Acquisition: Using transport plan properties as novel uncertainty signals for perturbation selection
  • Mutual Information Maximization: Adapting information-theoretic criteria to high-dimensional transcriptomic effect spaces
  • Interaction Discovery: Extending active selection to guide pairs for synergistic effect identification
  • Practical Deployment: Developing plug-and-play policy APIs for immediate lab adoption

Applications

Functional genomics, drug target discovery, genetic interaction mapping, and personalized medicine—enabling large-scale perturbation studies at a fraction of current costs while accelerating therapeutic development.

Unifying Vision

While addressing different scientific domains, both research thrusts share a common philosophy: AI should not merely predict but should reason within scientific constraints. In simulation, this means respecting physical laws; in experimental design, this means optimizing information gain under resource constraints. By developing AI systems that understand and operate within these fundamental boundaries, I aim to create the next generation of scientific tools that are not just powerful but also trustworthy, interpretable, and practically deployable.

The ultimate goal is to establish principled frameworks for AI-driven scientific discovery that can be adapted across multiple domains, accelerating our understanding of complex systems from the molecular to the planetary scale.

Research Directions

Direction 1: Provably Constrained Probabilistic Operators for Scientific Discovery

Research Core: Embedding physical constraints directly into neural operator architectures to create models that predict while respecting physics.

Phase 1: Foundation & The Tractable Testbed

  • Focus Areas: Hard-constrained architectures, probabilistic operator learning, generative models for PDEs
  • Physics: 2D Incompressible Navier–Stokes Equation with mass conservation (∇·u = 0)
  • Goal: Learn probabilistic operators that map initial conditions to future states while provably conserving mass

Phase 2: The Hard-Constrained Deterministic Core

  • Development of Divergence-Free Fourier Neural Operator (DF-FNO)
  • Architectural guarantees replacing soft physics penalties with exact conservation
  • General methodology for physically consistent deep learning architectures

Phase 3: The Probabilistic Leap

  • Coupling divergence-free operators with Conditional Variational Autoencoders (cVAE)
  • Generative modeling with physically valid uncertainty quantification
  • Paper: "Sampling from the Physical Manifold: Probabilistic Neural Operators with Hard Constraints"

Phase 4: Demonstration & Abstraction

  • Generalization to Wave Equation and Darcy Flow
  • Open-source library of constraint-enforcing neural layers
  • Interpretable latent dimensions for physical mechanisms

Direction 2: Closed-Loop Active Design for Genome-wide Perturb-seq

Research Extension:Optimal transport and mutual information frameworks for cost-effective perturbation discovery in single-cell genomics.

Aim 1: Novel Acquisition Functions for Perturbation Selection

  • Bayesian encoder-decoder trained on Replogle CRISPRi data
  • OT-based acquisition: Using transport-mass entropy and displacement norms as uncertainty signals
  • Mutual information acquisition: BatchBALD adaptation for transcriptomic effect vectors
  • Novelty: First use of OT plan properties to drive active selection in Perturb-seq

Aim 2: Standardized Benchmark for Active Perturb-seq Design

  • Offline "counterfactual" benchmark using Replogle 2022 data
  • Stratified splits by pathways and gene essentiality
  • Area Under Learning Curve (AULC) metrics for R² and DE recall
  • Comparison: OT-acquisition vs. Bayesian BALD vs. core-set k-center

Aim 3: Guide-Pair Selection & Practical Deployment

  • Extension to guide-pair acquisition for interaction discovery
  • Information gain maximization for synergistic effects
  • Development of "Policy API" for plug-and-play experimental design
  • Budget-aware ranking of perturbations without wet-lab integration

Broader Impact & Vision

My research advances AI as a fundamental instrument for scientific discovery. In scientific simulation, enforcing physical laws within neural architectures enables trustworthy, interpretable surrogates that accelerate climate modeling, fluid mechanics, and materials design. In experimental design, AI-driven active learning reduces the cost of genomic screens by orders of magnitude, making large-scale perturbation studies accessible to more labs and accelerating therapeutic target discovery.

The unifying vision is to develop AI systems that not only predict but also reason and design within scientific constraints, bridging data-driven learning with domain knowledge to solve previously intractable problems in both physical and biological sciences.
