Research Direction: AI for Scientific Discovery

Research Overview

My research lies at the intersection of machine learning, physics, and scientific computing — an emerging domain known as AI for Scientific Discovery. I develop provably constrained probabilistic operator models that enable AI systems to learn from and reason about complex physical systems while remaining faithful to the laws of science.

The central goal of my work is to bridge the gap between data-driven learning and physics-based reasoning. Most existing neural operator frameworks learn powerful function mappings but often violate the governing conservation laws that make physical systems predictable and interpretable. My research introduces architectural innovations that make such violations impossible by design — enabling the next generation of data-efficient, trustworthy, and interpretable surrogates for large-scale scientific simulations.

Thesis Topic

Provably Constrained Probabilistic Operators for Scientific Simulation

By embedding physical constraints directly into the architecture of probabilistic operator models, I aim to create machine learning systems that not only predict but also respect the physics governing their domain. This ensures that learned models obey conservation laws — such as mass, energy, or symmetry — while providing calibrated uncertainty quantification over possible physical outcomes.

Research Roadmap

Phase 1 — Foundation & The Tractable Testbed

Establishes a rigorous understanding of the literature and builds reproducible baselines for learning partial differential equations (PDEs) under uncertainty.

  • Focus Areas: Hard-constrained architectures (divergence-free nets, Hamiltonian NNs), probabilistic operator learning (FNO, DeepONet), and generative models for PDEs (Diffusion, VAEs).
  • Physics Selected: The 2D Incompressible Navier–Stokes Equation — a benchmark problem with mass conservation (∇·u = 0) and well-characterized turbulence dynamics.
  • Goal: Learn a probabilistic operator that maps distributions of initial conditions to distributions of future states while provably conserving mass.

Phase 2 — The Hard-Constrained Deterministic Core

Develops a deterministic neural operator architecture that enforces physical constraints exactly. This phase introduces the Divergence-Free Fourier Neural Operator (DF-FNO), which predicts the stream function ψ instead of raw velocity components. This ensures the resulting velocity field satisfies ∇·u = 0 by construction.

  • Contribution: Demonstrates how soft physics penalties (like PINO) can be replaced by architectural guarantees that enforce conservation to machine precision.
  • Outcome: A general methodology for building physically consistent deep learning architectures.

Phase 3 — The Probabilistic Leap

Extends the deterministic foundation into the probabilistic domain by coupling the divergence-free operator with a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE). This creates a generative model capable of sampling multiple physically valid solutions corresponding to uncertain or ambiguous initial conditions.

  • Innovation: Every generated sample adheres to the governing PDE constraints, providing uncertainty quantification without compromising physical validity.
  • Paper Title: “Sampling from the Physical Manifold: Probabilistic Neural Operators with Hard Constraints.”

Phase 4 — Demonstration & Abstraction

This final phase generalizes the methodology beyond fluid dynamics to other physical systems, such as the Wave Equation and Darcy Flow, and delivers open-source infrastructure to enable broader adoption of constraint-preserving learning.

  • Deliverable: A lightweight library of constraint-enforcing neural layers (e.g., DivergenceFreeOutput, SymmetricOutput, ExactBoundaryCondition).
  • Interpretability Goal: Analyze latent dimensions in the generative models to identify physically interpretable mechanisms (e.g., vortex strength or energy transfer).
  • Thesis Output: A unified framework for Provably Constrained Probabilistic Operators — merging determinism, uncertainty, and physics-based guarantees.

Broader Impact & Vision

This research advances a new paradigm where AI becomes a scientific instrument — not just a prediction tool. By enforcing the laws of nature within neural architectures, these models can accelerate scientific discovery, reduce the cost of simulations, and enhance trust in AI-driven decision-making for domains such as climate modeling, fluid mechanics, and materials design.

The ultimate vision is to contribute to the broader field of Scientific Machine Learning (SciML) by establishing principles for physically grounded AI — models that not only perform but also explain their reasoning through scientific structure.
